# Chapter Twenty-One: The Wall

The car's tail-lights turned out of the street and were gone.

Suraj stood at the open gate a moment longer, listening to the ordinary sounds of a city that had no idea what had just left it - a dog somewhere, a night bus changing gears two streets over - and then he turned back into the compound, alone, and did the thing he had already decided, some hours before tonight, that he was going to do.

He chose the pillar beside the gate rather than the compound wall itself - solid brick, unpainted, close enough to where a struggle would plausibly have happened. He did not allow himself a moment to reconsider it. Reconsidering was a luxury for a decision still being made, and this one had stopped being made a long time before tonight, in a study with the lamp still burning and a ledger that had, in the end, only ever balanced one way.

He struck his head against it once, hard, and the night went white at the edges.

He struck it again.

The second time drew blood, a thin line down past his temple that reached his jaw before he'd properly registered the warmth of it. He struck it a third time anyway, because a man who had merely stumbled once didn't bruise convincingly enough for what he needed Niraj to believe, and because some distant, still-functioning part of him understood that the pain, arriving late and enormous behind the third blow, was doing him the favour of drowning out everything else he might otherwise have had to feel in this exact moment.

He stood with one hand against the pillar afterward, breathing hard, blood reaching his collar now, and did not think about Ananya's face in the car's rear window, or about what he would tell Sandhya in the morning, or about anything at all beyond the single, narrow task still in front of him. There would be time for the rest of it later, whatever was left of him by the time later arrived.

He called Niraj from the gate, his voice unsteady in a way that required no performance at all.

"She's gone." He let the words come out ragged, which cost him nothing to fake, because they were true. "Shaktiyuth. He came for her. I tried to stop him at the gate - I couldn't -"

"Suraj. Suraj, slow down. Are you hurt?"

"It doesn't matter. Niraj, they can't have gone far. The forest road, I think, I heard the car turn that way -" He let his voice break properly there, once, and heard Niraj's breathing change on the other end of the line.

Niraj and Ankush reached the apartment inside twenty minutes, having been no further than the hotel three streets over where the wedding party had been lodged for the week, and had not come alone - Niraj's own car first, and close behind it three more, carrying most of the men Wolverine Industries kept on hand for exactly this kind of errand, brought along for the wedding's security and now, with a single phone call, repurposed for something considerably less ceremonial. Suraj was still standing at the gate when Niraj's car pulled in, blood dried dark along his jaw by then, one eye beginning to swell where the pillar's edge had caught it on the last strike. Ankush was out of the car before it had fully stopped.

"What happened. Tell me exactly what happened."

"He came over the side wall." Suraj kept his voice low, exhausted, exactly the register of a man who had already told this story once tonight and found no relief in the telling. "I heard something, went out to check - by the time I reached them she was already in the car. I tried to hold the gate. He's stronger than he looks."

"The side wall." Niraj said it slowly, turning to look at the wall in question, a good eight feet of plain brick with nothing obliging near it to climb from. "A man scales that, alone, in the dark, quietly enough that no one in the house heard a thing until you did?"

"I don't know how he did it. I only know that he did." Suraj let the exhaustion in his voice do the work his answer couldn't, and held perfectly still under the look Niraj gave him next - the blood, the swelling eye, the general disarray of a man who had genuinely, visibly fought for something and lost - understanding that this single moment, more than anything he'd say aloud, was the actual proof Niraj had come here to collect.

"You should sit down," Niraj said finally. "You look half dead."

"Later." Suraj straightened, wincing convincingly at the movement, which required no effort at all, since the wincing wasn't invented. "Niraj - they went toward the forest road. If you leave now -"

He didn't need to finish the sentence. Niraj was already turning, already calling out to the men who'd come in the three cars behind his own, eleven of them in all, his voice gone flat and businesslike in a way Suraj recognised as the sound of a decision being executed rather than considered.

"Ankush. Take those three cars, and every man in them." Niraj's hand found his son's shoulder, gripping hard enough that Ankush went briefly still under it. "Bring her back. Whatever it costs, whatever you have to do to that man to manage it - bring her back to this house tonight."

"And if he won't let her go?"

Niraj's face, in the security light, showed Suraj something he would remember for a long time afterward - not a father's fear for his son's safety, which might have given him a moment's pause about what he'd just set in motion, but something colder and more certain than that, a man who had never once in his life imagined an outcome where his own family didn't win.

"Then he won't be in a position to argue about it afterward," Niraj said. "Go."

Ankush hesitated one moment longer than the order strictly allowed for, some private calculation crossing his face that had nothing to do with fear of the man he was being sent after - closer, though Suraj was in no state that night to name it properly, to a young man being handed, yet again, a fight he'd never once been asked whether he actually wanted. Then he went. All three cars pulled out fast enough to scatter gravel across the drive, and were gone down the same street the first car had taken, into the same dark the night had already swallowed everything else into. Niraj's own car, and its driver, stayed where it had stopped, near the gate.

Suraj stood at the gate until the sound of the engines had faded entirely, and then a while longer after that, in a silence that had nothing left in it to disguise anything from him. His head had begun, in the particular way of an injury deciding it was finally owed some attention, to hurt in earnest. He let it. It seemed, on the whole, like the smallest part of what tonight was going to cost him, and he had never once, in thirty years of running a business built on knowing exactly what a thing was worth, been in the habit of flinching from an honest price.

He went inside, finally, to wash the blood from his face before Sandhya woke and found it first. In the bathroom mirror the swelling had already begun to darken properly around his eye, an honest wound doing honest work, and he studied it for a moment with something that was not quite satisfaction and not quite regret - the particular, unnamed thing a man feels looking at proof that he has, at last, actually paid for something rather than merely owing it.

Somewhere behind him, down a dark road neither of them had chosen, his daughter was still travelling further away from this house with every minute he stood here. Suraj found, wiping the last of the blood from his jaw, that this was the one part of the whole night's arithmetic he did not regret in the slightest, and did not expect he ever would. What he could not do, however he tried, was finish the other sum sitting underneath it - whether the man his daughter had chosen would still be capable of protecting her by the time whatever was happening out there in the dark had run its course. He had staked everything on a read of a stranger's character built from a newspaper article and a handful of secondhand stories. It was, he admitted to the mirror, not nothing. It was also, he admitted in the same breath, not a certainty, and he stood there a while longer with that particular uncertainty for company, since there was nothing else left tonight for him to do with it.
